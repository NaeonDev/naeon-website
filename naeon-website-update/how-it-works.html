The core

Every company

What every NAEON company has from the first day.

Sign-in for everyone

Office staff get an email invitation; factory and warehouse staff get a personal activation code. Management sign in with two-factor authentication, and a phone can be unlocked with a quick PIN.

Roles and permissions

Each person sees only what their role allows. Start from NAEON's ready-made roles for your type of company and adjust them.

Departments and sites

Your departments and branches, so requests reach the right manager and figures add up per site.

The approvals inbox

Every request waiting for someone lands in one inbox, on any device. Nobody can approve their own request; routine items can be approved in bulk; a request can be withdrawn, fixed and resubmitted, showing what changed.

A trail behind every entry

Created, approved or rejected, carried out and completed: who did it and when. The audit log keeps sensitive views and changes too.

Notifications

The bell in the app and push notifications, chosen per person and per type, with quiet hours at night. Approvals and security alerts always come through.

Your company, your look

Your logo and colour, light or dark mode, and a data-light mode for people on small data bundles.

Profile and devices

Each person's own details, their signed-in devices, a download of their own data and a request to remove their account.

How work moves through NAEON

1Captured

Someone records it where the work happens: a quote request at the customer, a fuel slip at the pump, a load update at the border.

2Approved

It goes to the right person's approvals inbox. They approve or reject it, with a reason.

3Carried out

The person who acts on it records that it was done, for example finance sending the quote.

4Completed

The record closes, and its trail shows who did each step and when.

NAEON doesn't replace your accounting system. Tax invoices, credit notes and statements stay there; finance takes the approved figures across with import-ready files.

On the road

Sales rep

A rep's day runs on their phone, from the first customer to the last follow-up.

  1. Clocks in on the phone. With Rep tracking on, they read the tracking notice once and kilometres are recorded only while they're clocked in.
  2. Opens today's visit plan and checks in at the customer. Away from the site, the check-in asks for a reason.
  3. Captures a quote request with lines from the price list, and sees what's in stock and what's on order.
  4. The request goes to the sales manager, and to management where your route says so. When finance sends the quote, the rep is told to follow up.
  5. Logs the call or visit on the customer's timeline, and watches the monthly target on their home.

Built in: A rep sees their own figures only, never cost prices or the gross margin.

In the office

Finance

Finance gets work that's already approved, with the trail behind it.

  1. Receives signed-off quote requests, sends the quote and records it, so the rep is told.
  2. Records customer payments and part payments, and follows up outstanding totals and accounts on hold.
  3. Approves expense claims and fuel slips after the line manager, and reimburses them in bulk.
  4. Takes approved claims and fuel into Sage, Xero or another system with import-ready files, never twice.
  5. Downloads the payroll export file for the pay period, in the company's own layout.

Built in: Nobody approves their own claim, finance included.

In the office

HR

HR keeps the people side in one place instead of a filing cabinet and a spreadsheet.

  1. Adds people, or imports the staff list, and gives factory staff a personal activation code.
  2. Decides leave against balances and the leave calendar, with public holidays, half days and clash warnings.
  3. Files contracts, IDs and certificates, with alerts before they expire.
  4. Publishes policies and sees who has read and acknowledged them.
  5. Runs shifts, swaps and overtime pre-approval, training and onboarding checklists, where those add-ons are on.

Built in: Sensitive views, like disciplinary records, are recorded every time someone opens them.

On a shared tablet

Factory floor

Operators don't need their own device or email address. They share a tablet at the line.

  1. Taps in on the shared tablet with their employee number and PIN, and clocks in at the tablet's site.
  2. Opens My jobs, starts a job and works through its steps, with the work instruction and current drawing on screen.
  3. Records output and scrap as they go. Scrap above the limit goes to the supervisor to approve.
  4. Reports a breakdown: the machine shows as down, a work order is raised, and maintenance and the supervisor are told.
  5. Is signed out after each turn, so the next person taps in.

Built in: Quality control never inspects output they produced themselves.

At the gate

Security at the gate

The gate knows who is on site, who is expected and what may leave.

  1. Logs a walk-up visitor; the person they're visiting approves from their own home screen.
  2. Checks contractors against the approved list and their induction before letting them in.
  3. Logs trucks in and out, and confirms that a loaded dispatch truck may leave, or stops it with a reason.
  4. Clocks employees in and out at the gate as their attendance.
  5. Starts an emergency roll call and ticks people off at the assembly point.

Built in: Visitor names stay out of the audit trail, and visitor records are deleted after your chosen period.

Logistics

Controller / dispatcher

The controller books, plans and follows every load, and keeps the customer informed.

  1. Books a load for a customer with its pickups and drop-offs, from saved places. The rate comes from the customer's rate card for that lane.
  2. Plans the day on the planning board: loads onto trucks, trailers and drivers, part loads, driver changeovers and backhaul suggestions. A truck that's off the road or a driver with expired documents is flagged before it goes.
  3. Follows the live board, with each load's last position, estimated arrival and late alerts.
  4. Shares a track-and-trace link with the customer, who sees the load's progress and POD but never the driver's details.
  5. Closes the load at POD received, and the Unbilled loads list shows finance what to invoice.

Built in: A rate changed beyond the allowed margin waits for sign-off, and nobody signs off their own.

Logistics

Driver

Everything the driver needs is on one phone screen, next stop first.

  1. Does the vehicle check before use. A major defect with a photo takes the truck off the road.
  2. Starts the trip. With Truck tracking on, positions are recorded only while the trip runs, after the driver has acknowledged the tracking notice.
  3. Presses Navigate to open the stop in Google Maps or Waze. Near the stop, the screen asks whether to mark Arrived.
  4. Records proof of collection (pallets, seal numbers, weighbridge ticket, photo) and, at the drop-off, the POD with the receiver's name and signature. Without signal, it's kept on the phone and sent later.
  5. On a cross-border load, records each crossing step in order. In trouble, a press-and-hold panic button alerts the company's nominated people with the truck's position. It doesn't call the police or armed response.

Built in: A driver never sees rates, prices or other drivers' loads.

Logistics

Fleet controller

The fleet controller keeps trucks, trailers and drivers legal and on the road.

  1. Keeps vehicles and trailers with their licence, COF and service reminders, and drivers' licences and PrDPs.
  2. Reviews each trip's track and the kilometres against the odometer. Only the fleet controller and management see tracks, and every view is recorded.
  3. Follows driver hours and behaviour scores, and decides what to do about a flag. NAEON never decides about a person by itself.
  4. Nominates traffic fines to the driver on the trip at the time, and follows accident and cargo claims, tyres and diesel.
  5. Builds the RTMS audit pack for a period and site.

Built in: Passport, visa and work-permit numbers are shown only on request, and every view is recorded.

Everywhere

Management

Management sees the whole company without chasing anyone for an update.

  1. Opens the management home: approvals waiting, sales, cash collected, staff on site and each department's figures.
  2. Approves from the phone, one at a time or routine items in bulk, with flagged ones held back.
  3. Opens reports for any area, compares periods and exports them, including profit per load, truck and customer for transporters.
  4. Is told when something needs them, through the company's cross-department alerts.
  5. Reads the Monday summary of last week, if the optional AI weekly summary is on.

Built in: Even management can't approve their own request.
