This policy explains how NAEON handles personal information when providing its business operations platform, website, trials, support and related services. It should be read with the applicable customer Terms of Service and Operator Agreement. It does not override statutory rights or relieve either party of duties imposed by law.

1. Who we are and who is responsible

NAEON supplies a multi-tenant software-as-a-service platform to business customers (Customers). The Customer generally determines why and how employee, contractor, visitor, supplier and end-customer information is processed within its workspace. For that information, NAEON ordinarily acts as an operator under the Customer’s documented instructions and the Operator Agreement.

NAEON is the responsible party for processing whose purposes it independently determines, including its own sales enquiries, customer account contacts, billing, contracts, website enquiries, security administration, support administration and certain platform-operational records. The legal role depends on the particular processing activity; neither label applies indiscriminately to every record.

Privacy and PAIA enquiries are handled by Lené van Wyk, Director and NAEON’s Information Officer, at privacy@naeon.co.za; Ulrich van Wyk, Director, is the Deputy Information Officer. NAEON will maintain the statutory Information Officer designation and registration records required by law. This notice does not represent that registration has been completed.

2. Information handled

Depending on enabled modules and customer use, Customer Data may include names, contact and employee identifiers, positions and access permissions; attendance, shifts, leave and training; PPE, assets and safety incidents; sales, suppliers, quotes, orders, warehouse, manufacturing, quality, deliveries, maintenance and fleet records; expense claims, fuel slips, photographs and documents; visitor names, arrival/departure times and vehicle plates; work messages and uploaded files; and GPS points where location features are enabled.

For transport and logistics customers, Customer Data may also include loads, stops, places and customer references; proof of collection and proof of delivery (photographs, signatures, names of the people who received goods, seal numbers and weighbridge tickets); driver and crew documents, including passport, visa and work-permit numbers; traffic fines; accident and cargo claims; driver behaviour events and scores; driving and rest hours; location-check findings; panic alerts; trip advances, allowances and incentive pay; border crossings, clearing references and cargo documents; and trip positions with speed and heading.

NAEON also handles login identifiers, authentication and MFA information, device and IP information, security events, access logs, support correspondence, billing contacts, trial and prospect information, website requests and technical diagnostics. Passwords are managed through authentication systems rather than stored as readable passwords by NAEON.

Customer uploads may contain health information (such as sick notes), disciplinary records, or other sensitive information. Disciplinary records are not automatically special personal information merely because they concern employment; their content determines whether special protections apply. Customers must assess lawful grounds and necessity before collecting or uploading sensitive information.

We do not design the platform to solicit unnecessary sensitive information. However, NAEON cannot guarantee that a Customer will never upload such information. Customers must configure permissions and use modules lawfully.

3. Purposes and lawful processing

When acting as operator, NAEON processes Customer Data to provide contracted features, authenticate authorised users, store and retrieve records, calculate and display operational information, route approvals, produce documents and reports, support the Customer, secure the platform, provide enabled notifications and facilitate authorised exports and deletion. NAEON does not sell Customer Data or use it to target advertising.

When acting as responsible party, NAEON processes contact and account information to respond to enquiries, manage commercial relationships, deliver services, maintain accounts and billing, meet legal and tax obligations, secure and troubleshoot its systems, investigate misuse, and establish or defend legal claims. Depending on the circumstances, the lawful ground may be contract, legal obligation, consent, or a legitimate interest recognised under section 11 of POPIA. Contractual necessity is not assumed merely because a person’s employer contracts with NAEON.

Any additional use of identifiable Customer Data beyond documented customer instructions requires an appropriate legal basis and, where necessary, updated contractual instructions and notices. Aggregated statistics may be used for service performance only where genuinely de-identified so that persons and customers cannot reasonably be re-identified.

4. Customer and user responsibilities

The Customer is responsible for giving legally required notices to its employees and other data subjects, identifying lawful grounds for its processing, configuring user permissions, choosing appropriate retention settings, addressing data subject requests, and ensuring the accuracy and lawful collection of its records. NAEON supplies tools and reasonable assistance in accordance with its Operator Agreement.

Users must protect login credentials, use their own accounts and report suspected unauthorised access. Customers should promptly revoke access when a person changes roles or leaves.

5. Location tracking and visitor records

GPS tracking is an optional feature for authorised staff such as sales representatives and drivers. NAEON’s server accepts location points only while a sales representative has an open clock-in session or while a driver has an active trip (from trip start to trip end), and rejects points outside those periods. Before anyone is tracked, the app shows NAEON’s tracking notice and records the person’s acknowledgement with its date and version; the notice is shown again when its wording changes, and the person can withdraw from their profile, which stops tracking at once and tells the Customer’s HR and the person’s manager. The application performs automatic clock-out checks at five-minute intervals for users who forget to clock out. A visible in-app indicator and device permissions support transparency; customers must ensure employees are informed before activation.

The Customer must document an appropriate POPIA lawful basis and assess whether tracking is necessary and proportionate. An employee’s device permission or acknowledgement is not, by itself, proof of legally valid consent. Where consent is the lawful basis, it must be valid and capable of withdrawal. Customers should offer an appropriate alternative where required and investigate disputed or inaccurate clock-in records.

For drivers, the phone may also send its speed, heading and accuracy with each position and, once the NAEON phone apps are released, signals that the phone’s location may be simulated or that the phone is jailbroken or rooted, and motion readings for harsh braking. On a cross-border trip, positions are recorded in other countries while the trip runs; they are stored in London like other Customer Data. Driver behaviour scores, driving-hours flags and location-check findings (readings that look impossible or simulated) are shown to the Customer’s authorised people for review; NAEON makes no automatic decision about anyone because of them. Passport, visa and work-permit numbers are visible only to the Customer’s fleet controller, HR and management roles, and every view is recorded.

Only the employee and authorised managers may view tracking records, and manager views are logged. GPS points and phone signals are subject to automatic deletion after 12 months, or sooner under the Customer’s setting; related mileage totals may be retained with claims where justified. Visitor records are deleted according to the Customer’s setting, with a maximum configured period of 12 months. Customers should set shorter periods where appropriate.

A Customer that uses the optional track-and-trace link decides whether to share a link to a load with its own customer. The link shows only the Customer’s name and logo, the load and customer reference, the time of each completed step, the truck’s last known position while the load is active, the estimated arrival and the proof of delivery; it never shows the driver’s name, telephone number or other personal details, the vehicle registration or prices. Links are hard to guess, are not listed by search engines, expire after a set period after delivery (default 3 days) and can be switched off by the Customer. NAEON counts each opening of a link and limits how often it can be opened; its technical logs may record the IP address of the device that opened it. NAEON does not authorise covert tracking or tracking unrelated to legitimate work purposes.

6. Optional AI functions

Optional AI functions include document and fuel-slip reading, Ask NAEON and management summaries. AI is not enabled during free trials and requires a Customer administrator to accept the AI notice; that acceptance is logged.

Ask NAEON uses fixed, permission-checked server queries rather than unrestricted database queries. Information submitted for an AI task is limited by the requesting user’s permissions and the task. Anthropic provides the AI service, with processing involving the United States. Under the commercial API arrangement described by the provider, API inputs are not used to train its models. Supplier terms may prescribe limited operational or abuse-monitoring retention; NAEON does not promise that AI inputs are never retained by the provider.

AI-generated text, classifications and pre-filled values may be inaccurate. A person must review them before reliance or action. NAEON does not authorise solely automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on individuals.

Health information, sick notes and disciplinary material are not authorised inputs for optional AI functions. NAEON will not activate production AI access to these categories unless effective technical exclusions are in place. Customers control AI permissions and must not submit information they are not authorised to disclose.

7. Communications and notifications

NAEON sends operational emails such as invitations, password resets, service notices and security alerts through an email service provider. WhatsApp business alerts are not live at the effective date of this policy; if introduced, they will be optional and subject to appropriate notices and opt-in arrangements. Where a user switches on push notifications in the web app, notifications are delivered through the push service of that user’s browser (for example Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft), with the content encrypted so that the push service cannot read it; Apple and Google native push services will be used only when the relevant native apps launch. Notifications should avoid unnecessary sensitive information.

Electronic direct marketing by NAEON is conducted only where POPIA section 69 permits it, including applicable consent or the limited existing-customer exception. Every marketing message provides a practical, free opt-out mechanism. Operational service messages are not used as a pretext for marketing.

8. Service providers and disclosure

NAEON uses Supabase for database, authentication, file storage and database backups in London, United Kingdom; Vercel for application functions in London and website delivery through a global content-delivery network; Resend for system email (sending from Ireland, European Union); Sentry for technical error monitoring; and Anthropic for optional AI processing, which may occur in the United States. Email and diagnostic service processing may occur in the locations permitted by the relevant providers’ applicable arrangements. Meta WhatsApp and Apple/Google native-app push integrations are not active for customer use at this version date; web push works as described in section 7.

GitHub is used for source-code management and Bitwarden for credential management; these services do not hold Customer Data. NAEON nevertheless assesses operational account information and supplier access as part of its internal supplier governance.

Maps in the app use map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, which NAEON hosts in its own storage; no personal information is sent to a map provider. When a driver presses Navigate, the stop’s position is opened in the driver’s own maps application (for example Google Maps or Waze), whose provider then processes it under its own terms. Countries for border crossings are worked out from boundary data bundled with NAEON, without an outside service.

NAEON may disclose information to authorised service providers, professional advisers under appropriate confidentiality duties, and competent authorities where legally required. Any corporate transaction involving personal information must be handled lawfully and subject to appropriate protections. Customer Data is not disclosed for unrelated purposes merely because a disclosure would be commercially convenient.

Sub-operator appointments, material changes and applicable safeguards are governed by the Operator Agreement. NAEON will maintain an accurate supplier register and update this notice when relevant processing materially changes.

9. Transfers outside South Africa

Primary Customer Data hosting is in London, United Kingdom. Certain technical, communication and optional AI processing may involve other jurisdictions, including the United States and globally distributed services.

International transfers must comply with section 72 of POPIA. Depending on the circumstances, a transfer may rely on adequate protection through law, binding corporate rules or a binding agreement, or another ground expressly recognised by section 72. NAEON uses contractual and organisational measures appropriate to the transfer and the services involved. A customer’s subscription or an individual’s use of the platform does not, by itself, constitute blanket consent to all international transfers.

10. Security safeguards and incidents

NAEON uses database row-level security and company-linked access restrictions, server-side permission checks, multi-factor authentication for privileged actions, HTTPS encryption in transit and provider-managed encryption at rest. Access to production infrastructure is restricted to authorised personnel using individually protected accounts. Sensitive actions, including access to GPS and selected HR records, permission changes, exports, deletions and AI activity, are audited. Security controls are periodically reviewed and adjusted to the risks.

NAEON maintains operational logging, error monitoring and database backups. Separate file-backup, restoration-testing and uptime-alert arrangements are part of NAEON’s deployment and operational controls. The availability and scope of a particular backup or recovery function depend on the controls actually implemented at the relevant time; this Privacy Policy is not a disaster-recovery service-level guarantee.

NAEON applies reasonable, risk-appropriate technical and organisational safeguards and reviews them as the service changes. No security measure eliminates all risk. Where NAEON as operator has reasonable grounds to believe Customer Data has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, it will notify the Customer immediately in accordance with section 21(2) of POPIA and assist the Customer. Where NAEON is the responsible party, it will make the notifications required by section 22 as soon as reasonably possible, subject to lawful exceptions.

11. Retention, recovery and deletion

During an active subscription, retention depends on record category, the Customer’s lawful instructions and system settings. GPS points are automatically deleted after 12 months, and visitor records after the Customer-selected period of no more than 12 months. Uploaded files currently have a five-year default setting, which Customers must review for legal appropriateness; it is not a statutory rule or a guarantee that every file must be kept for five years. Other categories are governed by approved retention schedules and lawful customer instructions.

Unless a Customer changes them, the record-retention defaults are: financial records 7 years, employee records 3 years after the person leaves and audit records 7 years. Records and files are listed for review a month before deletion, and a Customer may keep an item longer with a recorded reason.

After termination of the Customer agreement, NAEON retains Customer Data in a restricted recovery state for a standard period of 90 days to permit authorised export or retrieval. At the end of that period, it deletes Customer Data from active systems, subject to applicable legal preservation requirements and the documented expiry of backups. A Customer may request earlier deletion where lawful. The signed Terms of Service and Operator Agreement govern the practical export and deletion process.

Longer retention is not automatic. Where a Customer requests it in writing and a lawful purpose exists, NAEON may separately agree to archive specifically identified business or financial records for a defined period not exceeding five years, but only once the archive service is implemented and expressly contracted. GPS points, health records, sick notes, disciplinary records and other HR records are excluded from this optional archive. A specific legal preservation requirement must be assessed separately and must not be treated as general permission to archive these categories.

An optional longer-term archive is available only if NAEON has expressly agreed to provide that service in writing and has implemented suitable encryption, physical security, key management, access logging, recovery and secure destruction procedures. It is not part of the standard subscription. Customers must export and retain their own records where they have independent statutory or contractual retention duties.

NAEON retains its own invoices and contracts for applicable statutory periods, generally five years where required by tax or accounting law, or longer where a specific legal duty applies. Trial copies, with everything in them, and the record of who signed in to a trial are deleted within 24 hours after the trial ends; a prospect’s contact details are removed after 24 months without activity, or sooner on request. Backup copies expire within 35 days. Security logs and email evidence are retained for documented, proportionate periods and deleted or de-identified when no longer lawfully needed. Deletion from backups occurs according to verified backup expiry and restoration procedures.

Information reaches NAEON directly from Customers, authorised platform users, prospective customers and website visitors, and indirectly from Customer-selected integrations, devices and uploaded records. Where NAEON processes information solely as an operator, the Customer determines whether the collection and any onward disclosure are lawful. NAEON does not independently verify every Customer-provided record.

12. Your rights and requests

Under POPIA, individuals may request confirmation of processing and access to their information, request correction or deletion where legally justified, object to qualifying processing, withdraw consent where consent is the lawful basis, and lodge complaints with the Information Regulator. Rights are subject to lawful limitations, identity verification and applicable PAIA procedures.

For information processed in a Customer workspace, contact the Customer first as the responsible party. If a request reaches NAEON, it will refer it to the relevant Customer and provide reasonable assistance without independently altering Customer Data contrary to lawful instructions. For information for which NAEON is the responsible party, email privacy@naeon.co.za.

Users can also download their own data (profile, attendance, leave, their own requests and their tracking record) and ask for their account to be removed from their profile in the app; a removal request goes to the Customer’s HR or administrator, who decides it under the Customer’s retention rules.

An account deactivation is not necessarily deletion of underlying work records, statutory records or audit trails. NAEON and the Customer will distinguish those processes and explain lawful retention where relevant. Requests for records may also be made under NAEON’s PAIA Manual. A person may complain directly to the Information Regulator at inforegulator.org.za; prior contact with NAEON is encouraged but is not a precondition.

13. Children, website technology and changes

NAEON is designed for business use and does not invite children to create independent consumer accounts. Customers must ensure any processing concerning persons under 18, including lawful young workers or dependants mentioned in records, complies with POPIA’s provisions on children’s information and any applicable exceptions or authorisations.

The application uses necessary cookies and device storage for authentication, security, preferences and offline functions. NAEON does not use advertising or cross-site behavioural tracking cookies as part of its stated service. Any website analytics or other non-essential technologies will be disclosed and managed appropriately if introduced.

NAEON may update this policy as its services, suppliers or legal duties change. Material changes affecting processing will be communicated in a reasonably prominent manner, with advance notice where practicable. Urgent changes needed for security or legal compliance may take effect sooner. The current version and effective date will be available at naeon.co.za.
