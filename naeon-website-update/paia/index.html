Manual prepared under section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000, as amended, for NAEON (Pty) Ltd (registration number 2023/639158/07).

1. Purpose and legal framework

This manual explains how to request records held by NAEON (Pty) Ltd (“NAEON”), the categories of records held, the processing of personal information, and the remedies available under the Promotion of Access to Information Act (“PAIA”) and Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (“POPIA”). It applies to records in NAEON’s possession or under its control, whether in paper or electronic form.

Under section 50 of PAIA, a requester must establish that a record is required for the exercise or protection of a right, comply with prescribed procedures, and satisfy the statutory grounds for access. The mere inclusion of a category below does not establish that a record exists or that it must be disclosed.

Public information ordinarily available without a formal PAIA application includes the NAEON website, published Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, and this manual. 6.1 Customer records held as operator

Customer-controlled information hosted in the NAEON SaaS platform is generally processed by NAEON as an operator on the subscribing customer’s instructions. Requests concerning that information should ordinarily be addressed to the relevant customer as responsible party. A request received by NAEON will nevertheless be assessed under applicable PAIA obligations; NAEON will not automatically refuse it. Where appropriate and lawful, NAEON will consult or notify the customer, apply third-party procedures and protect confidential information.

7. Processing of personal information (PAIA section 51 disclosures)

7.1 Purposes

NAEON processes personal information to provide, administer, secure, support and improve its services; manage accounts, authentication, trials, subscriptions and billing; respond to enquiries; manage employees and suppliers; comply with law; prevent abuse and investigate incidents; and, for customer-controlled information, carry out documented customer instructions. Optional AI, GPS and communication features are subject to applicable controls and notices.

7.2 Data subjects and information

Customer contacts and usersIdentity, contact, company, account, authentication, billing and support information.
Employees, applicants and contractors Identification, contact, recruitment, employment, payroll, performance and legally required information.
Customer employees and other platform subjects Customer-entered personnel, attendance, location, safety, operations, visitor and other records, depending on enabled modules.
Website visitors and prospects Enquiry details, correspondence, technical usage and cookie data as applicable.
Suppliers and business partners Business contact, contract, transaction and payment information.

7.3 Recipients and international transfers

Authorised customer users may receive customer-controlled records according to assigned permissions. Service providers include Supabase (database, authentication, files and backups hosted in London, United Kingdom), Vercel (application services with London-region functions and global content delivery), Resend (system email, sending from Ireland), Sentry (error monitoring), and Anthropic (optional AI functionality). Web push notifications, where a user switches them on, pass through that user’s browser push service (for example Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft) in encrypted form. Meta WhatsApp and Apple/Google native-app push services apply only if those integrations are activated. Maps use OpenStreetMap data hosted by NAEON; no personal information is sent to a map provider. Processing regions for some providers may vary. NAEON assesses international transfers against section 72 of POPIA and applicable safeguards.

7.4 Security and retention

Measures include HTTPS, provider-managed encryption at rest, customer tenant segregation with database row-level security, role-based access, server-side authorisation, privileged multi-factor authentication, restricted production access and logging of sensitive actions. NAEON reviews controls in line with risk and its security obligations.

Following termination, customer-controlled data is ordinarily available for recovery for 90 days and then scheduled for deletion, subject to lawful preservation duties and technical backup cycles. A longer encrypted offline archive may be arranged only on the customer’s written instruction for eligible business or financial records and where lawful. GPS, health, sick-note, disciplinary and other HR records are excluded from optional archival and are scheduled for deletion at the end of the 90-day recovery period, subject to non-waivable legal preservation obligations. During an active subscription, record-specific retention settings and legal requirements apply. NAEON’s own corporate and statutory records follow applicable legal retention rules.

7.5 Rights and correction

Data subjects may request access to, correction or deletion of their personal information, or object to certain processing, in accordance with POPIA. For customer-controlled data, NAEON will ordinarily refer or coordinate requests with the customer responsible party. Requests may be sent to privacy@naeon.co.za. The Privacy Policy explains these rights in more detail.

8. Requesting access to a record

  • Use the prescribed PAIA Form 2 (Request for Access to Record), available from the Information Regulator, and send it to privacy@naeon.co.za or deliver it to NAEON’s physical address.

  • Describe the record sufficiently, the form of access sought, and the right to be exercised or protected and why the record is required.

  • Provide sufficient evidence of identity and, where acting for another person, proof of authority. NAEON will request only information reasonably necessary for verification.

  • A requester unable to complete a written form because of disability or illiteracy may seek assistance in accordance with PAIA.

  • NAEON will assess the request, any prescribed fee, applicable third-party notification and grounds of refusal, and issue a written decision.

A decision is ordinarily required within 30 days, subject to any lawful extension of up to a further 30 days, with required notice and reasons. Third-party notice and representation periods apply where relevant. Failure to decide within the statutory period may constitute deemed refusal.

10. Refusal, third-party rights and remedies

Access may be refused where PAIA permits or requires refusal, including protection of third-party privacy, confidential commercial information, safety, legal privilege and other grounds in Part 3, Chapter 4. NAEON will consider severing exempt portions where feasible and any applicable mandatory public-interest disclosure. Third parties will be notified where required by PAIA.

NAEON has no internal PAIA appeal procedure. An affected requester or third party may complain to the Information Regulator or approach a competent court, within the applicable statutory period and subject to procedural requirements. The PAIA complaint period is generally 180 days from the relevant decision. Forms and guidance are available from the Information Regulator.

11. Availability and revision

This manual is available free of charge electronically on request at privacy@naeon.co.za and is published at naeon.co.za/paia. Inspection at NAEON’s address can be arranged during normal business hours. NAEON will review and update the manual when material particulars, processing activities or applicable law change. The operative version is the latest published version.

Publication of this manual does not waive any lawful ground of refusal or create rights beyond applicable legislation.
